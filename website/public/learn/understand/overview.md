# What iMBrace is

iMBrace is an enterprise AI platform. It gives a company AI agents that answer questions from
its own approved knowledge, software that reads documents and turns them into structured
records, and workflows that carry out everyday business processes. A company can run it fully
on its own servers, in a private cloud, or as SaaS. The company chooses where its data lives
and which AI model reads it.

## Intelligent and accurate

iMBrace pairs AI that understands with steps that are exact, so a whole business process runs
end to end, reliably.

- Agents understand what people ask and answer from the company's own approved knowledge. When
  something is not in that knowledge, the agent says so.
- Workflows carry out the work precisely: routing, checks, calculations and updates to other
  business systems, the same way every time.
- People make the decisions that matter: a workflow waits for the right person's approval, then
  carries on by itself.

Together they run real business processes, not just conversations.

## Why it is governed

An AI agent that works inside a company's systems needs clear controls. iMBrace builds them in.

- Access is layered: by team, so people only see the knowledge and data of the teams they
  belong to; by role, so a person's permissions match what they are meant to manage versus use;
  and, on Enterprise installations, by individual field on a record, so one board can show
  different detail to different readers.
- The platform keeps a record of activity across it - logins, file uploads, channel changes,
  and changes to a Data Board - each one timestamped and, where a person did it, attributed to
  them. On Enterprise installations, that record extends to AI agent activity too.
- A workflow can pause at a decision that matters and wait for a named approver - for hours
  or days if needed. The approver decides from where they already work: an email with Approve
  and Reject buttons, each opening a page where they confirm. Opening the link decides
  nothing. Once they confirm, the same workflow picks up on its own and finishes what it was
  doing. Who decided, what they decided, and when is recorded with it.
- These rules are enforced by the platform's own checks, not by anything typed into a prompt.
- The company decides where the platform runs and which AI model it uses, including a model
  that never leaves its own building.

## How the pieces fit together

Customers and staff reach the platform through a channel they already use: a chat widget,
WhatsApp, or another connected channel. An agent answers, backed by the company's approved
knowledge. When something needs doing, a workflow does it, reaching into the company's other
business systems as needed. Anything the platform pulls from a document, or produces as a
record, lands on a structured board - and that record is what makes the answer, or the action,
checkable afterwards. One set of governance rules sits across the whole loop.
